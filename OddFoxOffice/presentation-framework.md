# Presentation Excellence Framework

## 1. Structure: The Narrative Arc

- **Open with a hook** — A bold statement, surprising fact, or relatable pain point. The audience decides in the first 30 seconds whether to pay attention.
- **Establish the problem** before introducing the solution. Make the audience *feel* the problem.
- **Follow a clear arc**: Situation → Tension → Resolution. Every great presentation is a story.
- **One idea per slide.** If you need a second idea, make a second slide.
- **End with a clear takeaway** — What should the audience remember, feel, or do?

## 2. Storytelling Over Information

- **Show, don't tell.** Use concrete examples, anecdotes, and demos instead of abstract descriptions.
- **Make the audience the hero.** Frame the story around their problems and aspirations, not your product features.
- **Use "before and after" contrast** — Paint the painful "before" vividly so the "after" feels transformational.
- **Personal stories build trust.** Share the real journey — the failures, pivots, and breakthroughs.
- **Every slide should answer: "Why should the audience care right now?"**

## 3. Visual Design Principles

- **Less text, more impact.** If the audience is reading your slides, they're not listening to you.
- **Maximum 6 words in a headline.** Support with speaker notes, not bullet walls.
- **One visual per slide** — a photo, chart, diagram, or key number. Never decorative clip art.
- **Consistent visual language** — Same fonts, colors, and layout rhythm throughout.
- **Use whitespace aggressively.** Crowded slides signal unclear thinking.
- **Data should tell a story** — Highlight the insight, not the raw numbers.

## 4. Pacing and Flow

- **Vary the rhythm.** Alternate between high-energy and reflective moments.
- **Use transition slides** to signal topic shifts (a single word or image works).
- **The "breath" rule** — After every major point, give the audience a moment to absorb.
- **Build complexity gradually.** Start simple, layer in detail.
- **Target 1-2 minutes per slide** for narrative slides, faster for visual-only slides.

## 5. Emotional Engagement

- **Start with empathy.** Show you understand the audience's world before asking them to enter yours.
- **Contrast creates emotion** — juxtapose the frustrating status quo against the elegant solution.
- **Use specific numbers and names** — "3 weeks of review time" hits harder than "a long time."
- **Humor is a tool, not a requirement.** One genuine laugh is worth more than five forced jokes.
- **Vulnerability > polish.** Audiences connect with real humans, not polished pitches.

## 6. The "So What?" Test

Every slide must pass this test: If someone in the audience silently asks "So what?" — does the slide answer it? If not, cut it or reframe it.

## 7. Closing Strong

- **Don't fade out.** The last 60 seconds should be the most memorable.
- **Callback to the opening** — Create a full-circle moment.
- **End on a forward-looking note** — Leave the audience inspired about what's next.
- **One final slide, one clear CTA** — Don't dilute the ending with logistics.

---

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- Bullet point walls (death by PowerPoint)
- Reading slides aloud verbatim
- Feature lists without user context
- "About us" slides at the beginning (nobody cares yet)
- Apologizing ("I know this is a lot of text...")
- Generic stock photos that add nothing
- Cramming too much into the "just in case" appendix mindset
